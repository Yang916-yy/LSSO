\section{Introduction}

Self-attention is the dominant global token mixer in modern sequence and
vision encoders~\cite{vaswani2017attention,dosovitskiy2021vit}. For a single
head, it applies
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Attn}(Q,K,V)
  =\softmax\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_h}}\right)V.
  \label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
Its global, content-dependent routing is expressive, but the interaction space
grows with sequence length and the arithmetic remains quadratic in the token
count, even when IO-aware kernels avoid materializing the complete score
matrix~\cite{dao2022flashattention}.

A broader view treats sequence mixing as learning from the current context.
Attention itself can be written as a context-instantiated two-layer network,
\begin{equation}
  q_i \longmapsto Kq_i
  \longmapsto \softmax(Kq_i)
  \longmapsto V^\top\softmax(Kq_i),
  \label{eq:attention_dynamic_mlp}
\end{equation}
whose hidden width is the number of tokens. Test-time training (TTT) makes this
learning interpretation explicit: key--value pairs form a temporary dataset,
a compact inner model is updated on that dataset, and its adapted weights are
used to process the current context~\cite{sun2024ttt}. Recent visual evidence
shows that full-batch inner learning is effective for bidirectional vision and
that increasing inner-model capacity helps; it also reveals a central obstacle:
deeper inner models and additional inner epochs become difficult to optimize
and expensive to unroll~\cite{han2026vit3}.

This raises a different question from how to design a deeper inner network:
can the useful per-sample learning problem be structured so that its optimum is
obtained exactly, without choosing or unrolling a finite number of adaptation
steps? We answer affirmatively for a strongly convex low-rank learner. Given
$X\in\R^{N\times D}$, each head predicts
\[
  U(X)\in\R^{N\times r},
  \qquad
  C(X)\in\R^{N\times d_h},
\]
and returns
\begin{equation}
  Y=(\mu\I+\gamma UU^\top)^{-1}C,
  \qquad \mu>0,\quad \gamma\geq0.
  \label{eq:lsso_intro}
\end{equation}
We call this module the Learnable Structured Solve Operator (\LSSO).

The key to its interpretation is the rank-space variable
\begin{equation}
  Z^\star
  =(\I+\alpha U^\top U)^{-1}\alpha U^\top C,
  \qquad \alpha=\gamma/\mu.
  \label{eq:fast_weights_intro}
\end{equation}
It is not merely a Woodbury intermediate. It is exactly the unique minimizer
of the per-sample inner problem
\begin{equation}
  Z^\star
  =\arg\min_Z
  \left[
    \frac12\lVert Z\rVert_F^2
    +\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert UZ-C\rVert_F^2
  \right].
  \label{eq:inner_intro}
\end{equation}
Thus $Z^\star(X)$ is a set of temporary fast weights learned from the current
sample. The token output is their regularized residual readout,
\begin{equation}
  Y=\mu^{-1}(C-UZ^\star).
  \label{eq:residual_intro}
\end{equation}
The token-space equilibrium and rank-space learner are an exact primal--dual
pair, connected by $\mu Y=C-UZ^\star$ and
$Z^\star=\gamma U^\top Y$.

This perspective subsumes our other interpretations. Gradient descent on the
inner objective converges to $Z^\star$ for every safe step size; \LSSO\ computes
that infinite-step limit directly through an $r\times r$ symmetric
positive-definite solve. The Neumann series is recovered as the special
unit-step iteration under its stronger spectral condition. In token space the
same solution is the unique minimizer of a strongly convex energy and a
rational spectral filter that suppresses modes strongly predictable from the
sample-specific relation subspace. \LSSO\ therefore eliminates
\emph{optimization depth} for this shared linear inner learner. It does not
claim a closed-form solution for an arbitrary deep nonlinear inner network.

For spatial inputs, we introduce Rank-Rotary LSSO (\RRLSSO). It applies a
token-dependent orthogonal rotation $R(p_i)$ to each rank feature. The resulting
inner feature kernel is
\[
  u_i^\top R(p_i)^\top R(p_j)u_j
  =u_i^\top R(p_j-p_i)u_j.
\]
Beyond injecting relative displacement, the rotation changes the covariance,
conditioning, and effective rank of the per-sample regression design. It
therefore reshapes the geometry and effective adaptation directions of the
inner learner while preserving row norms, total relation energy, and the
positive-semidefinite Gram form.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate global token mixing as closed-form per-sample inner
  learning: the outer network constructs a low-rank ridge problem and an exact
  structured solve obtains its temporary fast weights.
  \item We prove an exact primal--dual equivalence between the token-space
  equilibrium and rank-space ridge learner, and identify the output as a
  regularized sample-conditioned residual.
  \item We show that the solve is the exact limit of convergent inner gradient
  descent, with Neumann expansion as a unit-step special case, thereby
  analytically eliminating finite-step inner optimization depth.
  \item We derive well-posedness, spectral, norm, conditioning, and energy
  guarantees, together with an exact Woodbury implementation that never forms
  an $N\times N$ matrix.
  \item We interpret rank rotation as inner-feature geometric augmentation and
  characterize how stable-rank redistribution changes adaptation directions
  and the convergence margin without changing total relation energy.
  \item We validate the operator without pretraining on eight genomic sequence
  classification tasks. A matched \RRLSSO\ encoder wins seven tasks, uses fewer
  parameters, and reduces mixer MACs by up to $12.08\times$ relative to MHA.
\end{itemize}
