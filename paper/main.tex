\title{LSSO: Certified Content-Conditioned Global Mixing with Accretive Equilibria}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Efficient sequence layers face a persistent three-way tension: global
bidirectional interaction, content-conditioned token mixing, and an explicit
stability certificate.  We introduce LSSO, a global low-rank mixer that
constructs a compact, sample-conditioned equilibrium rather than an unrolled
inner optimization.  Relation features define a QR soft frame, while a shared
content cross-moment generates a strictly accretive compact operator.  Its
reflected resolvent ranges over the full interior of the compact matrix ball;
a learned scalar complement then lifts it to token space without fixing the
unmodeled subspace to the identity.  In exact arithmetic, every frozen
input-conditioned token mixer is strictly contractive in $\ell_2$, and its
deviation from the scalar complement has rank at most the compact rank.  The
equilibrium is the exact infinite-time limit of a globally convergent contextual
inner flow, but is evaluated by one small direct solve.  No fixed-point
iteration, inner learning rate, unrolling, or test-time gradient update is part
of the forward pass.  In a matched eight-task GenomicBenchmarks comparison,
LSSO is higher than multi-head attention on seven tasks and improves unweighted
macro accuracy from $75.37$ to $78.53$.  Four Long Range Arena tasks further
test the operator on sequences of up to $4096$ positions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Global token interaction is useful in both sequence and vision encoders, but
the usual dense attention map has quadratic sequence cost.  Recent efficient
models explore several alternatives: fixed or low-rank attention
approximations, latent bottlenecks, and input-selective recurrent state
updates \cite{wang2020linformer,xiong2021nystrom,jaegle2021perceiver,
gu2022s4,gu2024mamba,dao2024mamba2}.  These approaches establish that the
design space is broader than dense attention, but they expose a different
tension.  A token mixer that changes with each sample is expressive, while a
token mixer with a simple, per-instance spectral certificate is easier to
reason about.  Obtaining both without reverting to an iterative implicit
solver is less direct.

This tension matters because the stability of an attention-like layer is not a
cosmetic property.  Standard dot-product self-attention is not globally
Lipschitz on an unbounded input domain \cite{kim2021lipschitz}.  Monotone and
equilibrium networks instead impose structure that gives existence and
stability guarantees \cite{winston2020mondeq}, but their parameters are
typically static and their forward computation is framed around a fixed-point
or splitting solver.  Meanwhile, test-time training (TTT) builds a compact
inner model from the current sequence through online optimization
\cite{sun2025ttt}; ViT$^3$ makes the associated inner-model design space
explicit for visual sequence modeling \cite{han2026vit3}.  These are useful
perspectives, but ViT$^3$ also documents the practical cost of inner training:
loss, learning rate, update schedule, and inner architecture all matter, while
deeper inner models face both outer-loop initialization and inner-loop gradient
difficulties.  More complete test-time optimization can improve sequence
models, but iterative solvers spend additional inference computation
\cite{vonoswald2026mesanet}.  This motivates a complementary question: when
the desired contextual state is structured, can its optimum be solved exactly
rather than approximated by a finite update rule?

We ask a narrower question:
\begin{quote}
Can a noncausal, global token mixer be conditioned on the current sample,
scale linearly with sequence length at fixed compact rank, and still carry an
explicit spectral certificate for every generated token kernel?
\end{quote}

LSSO answers this question with a compact matrix-ball construction.  Each head
first compresses content into a shared cross-moment $Z=P^\T C$.  In the
default \textsc{Dynamic} mode, $Z$ generates unconstrained compact coordinates
that are mapped to a strictly accretive matrix $K$.  A reflected resolvent
\begin{equation}
    M = 2(\I + K)^{-1}-\I
\label{eq:reflected-resolvent}
\end{equation}
is therefore a strict contraction.  LSSO does not materialize an $N\times N$
transition matrix.  Instead, a QR soft frame $P$ and a learned scalar
complement $\eta$ lift $M$ to
\begin{equation}
    T = \eta\I + P(M-\eta\I)P^\T.
\label{eq:token-mixer-intro}
\end{equation}
The complement is essential: on the token subspace not represented by $P$, the
mixer acts as $\eta\I$ rather than as a hard-coded identity.

The paper makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We define a content-conditioned global mixer with a QR soft frame,
    an accretive compact equilibrium, and a scalar-complement token lift.  The
    only public ablations are the compact-core ownership modes
    \textsc{Dynamic}, \textsc{Static}, and \textsc{Zero}, together with an
    optional rank-space phase rotation.
    \item We prove that the contextual inner flow converges globally and
    exponentially to the state returned by the direct solve.  We give the
    corresponding discrete gradient-descent limit, exact implicit derivative,
    and perturbation bounds; the production forward has no inner optimizer.
    \item We prove that the compact parameterization covers all real strictly
    accretive matrices and, through the Cayley map, all real strict compact
    contractions, and that every frozen, sample-conditioned token mixer is a
    strict $\ell_2$ contraction and satisfies
    $\rank(T-\eta\I)\leq r$.  We state precisely why this does \emph{not}
    imply a global Lipschitz bound for the complete dynamic neural block.
    \item In a three-seed matched comparison on eight genomic classification
    tasks, LSSO exceeds MHA on seven tasks and improves unweighted macro
    accuracy by $3.16$ percentage points.  We also report a four-task LRA
    panel, while separating our task-specific protocol from external
    literature values rather than treating them as an apples-to-apples rank.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Efficient global and selective sequence models.}
Low-rank attention methods such as Linformer and Nystr\"omformer reduce the
cost of dense attention through projection or landmark structure
\cite{wang2020linformer,xiong2021nystrom}.  Perceiver-style models use a
latent bottleneck for global exchange \cite{jaegle2021perceiver}.  State-space
and selective sequence models offer another route to long context, using
structured recurrences and input-dependent state evolution
\cite{gu2022s4,gu2024mamba,dao2024mamba2}.  LSSO is neither a softmax
approximation nor a causal scan.  It is a noncausal encoder mixer that forms
one global compact state per sample and head.  Its formal object is a
content-conditioned low-rank perturbation of a scalar complement, not an
arbitrary attention matrix.

\paragraph{Inner adaptation and test-time training.}
TTT interprets sequence processing through a compact inner model learned from
the sequence \cite{sun2025ttt}; ViT$^3$ shows that the choices inside that
inner model remain consequential in vision \cite{han2026vit3}.  Linear
attention also admits a fast-weight interpretation \cite{schlag2021fastweights},
and recent work derives recurrent updates from online or test-time regression
objectives \cite{liu2024longhorn,wang2025testtimeregression}.  Longhorn already
uses a closed-form associative-recall update, while MesaNet approximately
optimizes a causal in-context objective at every time point with a parallel
conjugate-gradient solver \cite{vonoswald2026mesanet}.  LSSO therefore does not
claim the first closed-form inner update or literal equivalence to a TTT layer.
Its contribution is a different combination for noncausal encoders: the whole
sample generates a complete strictly accretive compact system,
$U^\star=(\I+K)^{-1}Z$ is evaluated by a direct solve, and the resulting frozen
token kernel carries an explicit spectral certificate.  No learned inner-model
weights are updated per sequence.

\paragraph{Certified and implicit networks.}
Monotone operator equilibrium networks obtain well-posedness from monotone
operator structure \cite{winston2020mondeq}.  Our construction uses the same
classical accretive/resolvent geometry, but in a different regime: the compact
operator is generated from the current input, its equilibrium has a closed
form, and the certificate applies to the resulting frozen token kernel.  This
is intentionally weaker than a theorem about the full input-to-output
Jacobian of a dynamic network.  The distinction is important in light of the
non-Lipschitz behavior of ordinary self-attention
\cite{kim2021lipschitz}.

\section{LSSO}
\label{sec:method}

We describe one head and omit the batch index.  Let $X\in\R^{N\times D}$ be
the incoming token matrix, $d$ the head width, and $r$ the compact rank.  A
single input projection produces relation and content coordinates
\begin{equation}
    [A,C] = XW_{bc}+b_{bc},
    \qquad A\in\R^{N\times r},\quad C\in\R^{N\times d}.
\label{eq:input-projection}
\end{equation}
Heads are concatenated and passed through an ordinary output projection after
mixing.  The construction below is repeated independently for each head,
except that the same head's compact state couples all of its valid tokens.

\subsection{Masking and rank-space phase}
\label{sec:mask-and-phase}

For a boolean validity mask $m\in\{0,1\}^N$, invalid rows of both $A$ and $C$
are set to zero before any compact statistic is formed.  Let
\begin{equation}
    n = \sum_{i=1}^N m_i.
\end{equation}
For a nonempty sequence, relation coordinates are normalized as
$A\leftarrow A/\sqrt{n}$.  An entirely masked sequence is assigned the zero
output by definition.  This convention makes padding a semantic input rather
than a separate dense-only path.

The optional Rank-Rotary ablation applies a block-diagonal planar rotation to
the $r$ relation coordinates before the soft frame.  For centered position
$p_i$ and frequencies $\{\omega_j\}_{j=1}^{r/2}$, each coordinate pair uses
the angle $p_i\omega_j$.  The transformation preserves each relation row's
Euclidean norm and obeys the relative-phase identity
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal R(p_i)^\T\mathcal R(p_j)=\mathcal R(p_j-p_i).
\label{eq:rank-rotary-relative}
\end{equation}
It is therefore an internal phase choice in rank-feature space.  It is not a
RoPE replacement, an absolute-position representation, or a claim of
two-dimensional spatial equivariance.

\subsection{QR soft frame and compact statistic}
\label{sec:soft-frame}

The QR soft frame maps unconstrained relation coordinates to a strict column
contraction.  Form the reduced QR factorization
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}P\\E\end{bmatrix}R_{\mathrm{qr}}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}A\\\I_r\end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    \begin{bmatrix}P\\E\end{bmatrix}^{\!\T}
    \begin{bmatrix}P\\E\end{bmatrix}=\I_r,
\label{eq:soft-frame}
\end{equation}
where $P\in\R^{N\times r}$ is the token-space frame.  The implementation
applies a detached positive common scale before this QR; it leaves the exact
$Q$ factor unchanged and keeps the numerical evaluation in a normal range.
The identity augmentation means that the factorization is defined even when
$N<r$ or $A$ is rank deficient.

The frame compresses the content into one shared cross-moment,
\begin{equation}
    Z=P^\T C\in\R^{r\times d}.
\label{eq:cross-moment}
\end{equation}
Unlike a recurrent state, $Z$ summarizes all valid positions symmetrically in
one noncausal pass.  The token-level matrix in \eqref{eq:token-mixer-intro}
is conceptual only; it is never instantiated.

\subsection{Accretive compact equilibrium}
\label{sec:accretive-core}

In the default \textsc{Dynamic} mode, $Z$ generates compact raw coordinates
\begin{equation}
    \Theta = \Theta_0 + \frac{ZW_{\mathrm{drive}}}{\sqrt{n}},
\label{eq:dynamic-coordinates}
\end{equation}
where $\Theta_0\in\R^{r\times r}$ and
$W_{\mathrm{drive}}\in\R^{d\times r}$ are learned per-head parameters.  The
\textsc{Static} ablation uses $\Theta=\Theta_0$, and \textsc{Zero} owns no compact
coordinates.  No other core variants are part of the operator contract.

For \textsc{Dynamic} and \textsc{Static}, define
\begin{align}
    L(\Theta) & = \tril(\Theta,-1)+
        \diag\!\left(\operatorname{softplus}
        \bigl(\operatorname{diag}(\Theta)+c_1\bigr)\right), \\
    \Omega(\Theta) & = \triu(\Theta,1)-\triu(\Theta,1)^\T, \\
    K(\Theta) & = L(\Theta)L(\Theta)^\T+\Omega(\Theta),
\label{eq:accretive-parameterization}
\end{align}
where $c_1=\operatorname{softplus}^{-1}(1)$.  Thus $L$ is lower triangular
with positive diagonal, while $\Omega$ is skew-symmetric.  The compact
equilibrium and the pre-output token coordinates are
\begin{align}
    U^\star &=(\I_r+K)^{-1}Z, \\
    Y &= \eta C+P\left[2U^\star-(1+\eta)Z\right],
    \qquad \eta=\tanh(\eta_{\mathrm{raw}})\in(-1,1).
\label{eq:equilibrium-readout}
\end{align}
This is the exact-real map.  The floating-point implementation evaluates a
stable logistic identity and multiplies by one minus the calculation-dtype
machine epsilon, reserving one ULP from $\{-1,1\}$.  The complement is learned
independently for each head and is initialized nominally to $0.9$.  In \textsc{Zero},
we set the compact term to $M=0$, which gives
\begin{equation}
    Y=\eta(C-PZ).
\label{eq:zero-core}
\end{equation}
At $\Theta=0$, $L=\I_r$, $\Omega=0$, $K=\I_r$, and the compact contraction is
$M=0$.  Both nonzero-core modes start at that point; \textsc{Dynamic} also
starts with $W_{\mathrm{drive}}=0$.

For later use, define the reflected resolvent
\begin{equation}
    M = 2(\I_r+K)^{-1}-\I_r
      = (\I_r-K)(\I_r+K)^{-1}.
\label{eq:cayley}
\end{equation}
Substituting $Z=P^\T C$ into \eqref{eq:equilibrium-readout} gives the exact
token-mixing form
\begin{equation}
    Y=TC,\qquad
    T=\eta\I_N+P(M-\eta\I_r)P^\T.
\label{eq:token-mixer}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Solved contextual inner state.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:equilibrium-readout} is the exact solution of a small
linear system.  Section~\ref{sec:solved-inner-process} proves that it is the
infinite-time limit of a globally convergent contextual flow and, separately,
the infinite-step limit of gradient descent on a residual objective.  These
are mathematical interpretations, not the computation performed by LSSO.
In particular, when $\Omega\neq0$, the original equation
$(\I_r+K)U=Z$ is not the first-order condition of a ridge or other symmetric
convex energy in $U$.

\paragraph{Cost.}
For fixed $r$ and $d$, frame formation costs
$O(Nr^2)$ per head, the frame-content products cost $O(Nrd)$, and the compact
solve costs $O(r^2d+r^3)$.  The mixer is therefore linear in $N$ at fixed
compact dimensions and never stores an $N\times N$ token matrix.  The dense
input and output projections remain ordinary feature projections, as in other
token mixers.

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

All statements in this section are exact-real-arithmetic statements about a
single head after its input-conditioned quantities have been generated.  The
mixed-precision numerical contract is discussed separately in
Section~\ref{sec:theory-scope}; it is not a claim of bitwise strictness for arbitrary
floating-point inputs.

\subsection{The QR soft frame is a strict contraction chart}

\begin{theorem}[Soft-frame contract]
\label{thm:soft-frame}
Let $P$ be the upper block of the $Q$ factor in
\eqref{eq:soft-frame}.  Then
\begin{equation}
    P^\T P \prec \I_r,
    \qquad
    \I_N-PP^\T \succ 0.
\label{eq:soft-frame-contract}
\end{equation}
Moreover, at the level of represented frames, the construction covers every
$P\in\R^{N\times r}$ satisfying $P^\T P\prec\I_r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lower block of \eqref{eq:soft-frame} obeys
$ER_{\mathrm{qr}}=\I_r$.  Since the augmented matrix has full
column rank, $R_{\mathrm{qr}}$ and hence $E$ are nonsingular.  Orthogonality
of the full thin-$Q$ factor gives
\begin{equation}
    P^\T P+E^\T E=\I_r.
\end{equation}
Because $E^\T E\succ0$, the first claim follows.  In particular,
$\norm{P}_2<1$, which implies $PP^\T\prec\I_N$ and proves the second
claim.

For completeness, take any $P$ with $P^\T P\prec\I_r$ and write
$S=\I_r-P^\T P\succ0$.  Let $C$ be the upper-triangular Cholesky factor of
$S$, so $S=C^\T C$, and set $R_{\mathrm{qr}}=C^{-1}$ and
$A=PR_{\mathrm{qr}}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}A\\\I_r\end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}P\\R_{\mathrm{qr}}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}
    R_{\mathrm{qr}},
\end{equation}
and the left factor has orthonormal columns because
$R_{\mathrm{qr}}^{-\T}R_{\mathrm{qr}}^{-1}=S$.  This is a valid reduced QR
factorization with positive diagonal and produces the desired upper block.
\end{proof}

The identity augmentation is more than a numerical safeguard.  It supplies a
strict defect $\I_N-PP^\T$, which will let us complete the compact contraction
to token space without an uncontrolled residual subspace.

\subsection{The compact core parameterizes the strict matrix ball}

\begin{definition}[Strict accretivity]
\label{def:accretive}
A real square matrix $K$ is strictly accretive when
\begin{equation}
    \sym(K) \coloneqq \frac{K+K^\T}{2}\succ0.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Accretive Cayley parameterization]
\label{thm:accretive-cayley}
The map in \eqref{eq:accretive-parameterization} ranges over all real
strictly accretive $r\times r$ matrices.  For every such $K$, the reflected
resolvent $M$ in \eqref{eq:cayley} is a strict spectral-norm contraction and
obeys
\begin{equation}
    \I_r-M^\T M
    =4(\I_r+K)^{-\T}L L^\T(\I_r+K)^{-1}\succ0.
\label{eq:cayley-defect}
\end{equation}
Conversely, the map $K\mapsto M$ is a bijection from real strictly accretive
matrices to the open real matrix ball $\{M:\norm{M}_2<1\}$, with inverse
\begin{equation}
    K=(\I_r-M)(\I_r+M)^{-1}.
\label{eq:inverse-cayley}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The construction has
$\sym(K)=LL^\T\succ0$, because $L$ has positive diagonal.  Conversely, for
any strictly accretive $K$, let $L$ be the lower-triangular Cholesky factor of
$\sym(K)$ and let $\Omega=K-LL^\T$.  Then $\Omega^\T=-\Omega$.  The strict
lower, diagonal, and strict upper entries can independently be represented by
the raw coordinates in \eqref{eq:accretive-parameterization}; the softplus
diagonal maps onto $(0,\infty)$.  Thus the parameterization is complete.

Let $Q=\I_r+K$.  Strict accretivity implies that $Q$ is nonsingular.  Using
$M=(\I_r-K)Q^{-1}$ gives
\begin{align}
    \I_r-M^\T M
    &=Q^{-\T}\left[Q^\T Q-(\I_r-K)^\T(\I_r-K)\right]Q^{-1}\\
    &=4Q^{-\T}\sym(K)Q^{-1},
\end{align}
which is \eqref{eq:cayley-defect}.  It is positive definite, so
$\norm{M}_2<1$.

For the converse, $\norm{M}_2<1$ makes $\I_r+M$ nonsingular.  Substitution of
\eqref{eq:inverse-cayley} yields
\begin{equation}
    K+K^\T
    =2(\I_r+M)^{-\T}(\I_r-M^\T M)(\I_r+M)^{-1}\succ0.
\end{equation}
The two fractional-linear expressions are algebraic inverses wherever they
are defined, establishing the bijection.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closed-form strongly monotone equilibrium]
\label{cor:equilibrium}
For every $Z\in\R^{r\times d}$, the equilibrium
$U^\star=(\I_r+K)^{-1}Z$ exists uniquely.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{\min}(\I_r+K)\geq1.
\label{eq:solve-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a nonzero $v$,
\begin{equation}
    \inner{(\I_r+K)v}{v}
    =\norm{v}_2^2+\norm{L^\T v}_2^2>0,
\end{equation}
because the skew-symmetric part contributes zero.  Therefore
$\I_r+K$ is strongly monotone and nonsingular.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
    \norm{(\I_r+K)v}_2\norm{v}_2
    \geq \inner{(\I_r+K)v}{v}
    \geq\norm{v}_2^2,
\end{equation}
which proves \eqref{eq:solve-bound}.  Applying the inverse to each column of
$Z$ gives the unique equilibrium.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The direct solve is an infinite inner-process limit}
\label{sec:solved-inner-process}

Let $A_K=\I_r+K$.  The following result states precisely in what sense the
forward pass replaces an infinite contextual inner process.  It does not
identify LSSO with an arbitrary TTT objective or nonlinear inner model.

\begin{theorem}[Solved contextual inner process]
\label{thm:inner-process}
For fixed contextual quantities $K$ and $Z$, consider the continuous dynamics
\begin{equation}
    \dot U(t)=Z-A_KU(t).
\label{eq:contextual-flow}
\end{equation}
From every initial state $U(0)$, the dynamics converge to
$U^\star=A_K^{-1}Z$ and obey
\begin{equation}
    \norm{U(t)-U^\star}_F
    \leq e^{-t}\norm{U(0)-U^\star}_F.
\label{eq:flow-rate}
\end{equation}
The rate lower bound is independent of the skew-symmetric part of $K$.

The same state uniquely minimizes the residual objective
\begin{equation}
    J(U)=\frac12\norm{A_KU-Z}_F^2.
\label{eq:residual-objective}
\end{equation}
Let $s_{\min}$ and $s_{\max}$ be the extreme singular values of $A_K$.
Gradient descent on $J$ with a fixed step
$0<\alpha<2/s_{\max}^2$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \norm{U_{j}-U^\star}_F
    \leq \rho^j\norm{U_0-U^\star}_F,
    \qquad
    \rho=\max_i\left|1-\alpha\sigma_i(A_K)^2\right|<1.
\label{eq:residual-gd-rate}
\end{equation}
Thus the direct solve returns both the infinite-time limit of
\eqref{eq:contextual-flow} and the infinite-step limit of every such residual
gradient descent run, without choosing $\alpha$ or an update count in the
implemented forward pass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $E(t)=U(t)-U^\star$.  Since $A_KU^\star=Z$,
$\dot E=-A_KE$.  The skew part vanishes in the Frobenius inner product, so
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{dt}\frac12\norm{E}_F^2
    =-\inner{E}{A_KE}_F
    =-\inner{E}{\sym(A_K)E}_F
    \leq-\norm{E}_F^2,
\end{equation}
because $\sym(A_K)=\I_r+LL^\T\succeq\I_r$.  Gronwall's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:flow-rate}.

The Hessian of $J$ is $A_K^\T A_K\succ0$, so its unique stationary point
solves $A_K^\T(A_KU-Z)=0$ and hence equals $U^\star$.  For
$E_j=U_j-U^\star$, gradient descent gives
$E_{j+1}=(\I_r-\alpha A_K^\T A_K)E_j$.  Diagonalizing the symmetric positive
definite matrix $A_K^\T A_K$ proves \eqref{eq:residual-gd-rate} and the stated
step-size interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact implicit derivative and sensitivity]
\label{prop:implicit-gradient}
For perturbations of $A_K$ and $Z$, the equilibrium differential is
\begin{equation}
    dU^\star=A_K^{-1}\left(dZ-dA_K\,U^\star\right).
\label{eq:equilibrium-differential}
\end{equation}
If $G=\partial\mathcal L/\partial U^\star$ for a scalar outer loss and
$V=A_K^{-\T}G$, then
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial Z}=V,
    \qquad
    \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial A_K}=-VU^{\star\T}.
\label{eq:implicit-vjp}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    \norm{A_K^{-1}}_2\leq1,
    \quad
    \norm{U^\star}_F\leq\norm{Z}_F,
    \quad
    \norm{dU^\star}_F
    \leq\norm{dZ}_F+\norm{dA_K}_2\norm{Z}_F.
\label{eq:equilibrium-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The direct-solve backward therefore computes the exact implicit gradient of
the converged equilibrium rather than a finite-unroll gradient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating $A_KU^\star=Z$ gives
$dA_K\,U^\star+A_KdU^\star=dZ$, which proves
\eqref{eq:equilibrium-differential}.  Substitution into the Frobenius
differential of $\mathcal L$ and cyclicity of the trace give
\eqref{eq:implicit-vjp}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:equilibrium} gives
$\sigma_{\min}(A_K)\geq1$, hence $\norm{A_K^{-1}}_2\leq1$; applying this
bound to $U^\star=A_K^{-1}Z$ and
\eqref{eq:equilibrium-differential} yields
\eqref{eq:equilibrium-sensitivity}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Defect completion lifts the certificate to tokens}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional contraction and low-rank structure]
\label{thm:token-contraction}
Fix a sample and head, and let $P$, $M$, and $\eta$ be constructed as above.
Then the token mixer in \eqref{eq:token-mixer} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \norm{T}_2 <1,
    \qquad
    \rank(T-\eta\I_N)\leq r.
\label{eq:token-contract}
\end{equation}
For every $v\in\ker(P^\T)$, it further obeys $Tv=\eta v$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:soft-frame},
$\Delta=(\I_N-PP^\T)^{1/2}$ is well-defined and
\begin{equation}
    F=[P\;\Delta],\qquad FF^\T=\I_N.
\end{equation}
The token mixer admits the defect-completed factorization
\begin{equation}
    T
    =PMP^\T+\eta(\I_N-PP^\T)
    =F
      \begin{bmatrix}
          M & 0\\
          0 & \eta\I_N
      \end{bmatrix}
      F^\T.
\label{eq:defect-completion}
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:accretive-cayley} gives $\norm{M}_2<1$, while
$\eta=\tanh(\eta_{\mathrm{raw}})$ gives $|\eta|<1$.  Since $F$ is a
coisometry,
\begin{equation}
    \norm{T}_2
    \leq\max\{\norm{M}_2,|\eta|\}<1.
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
    T-\eta\I_N=P(M-\eta\I_r)P^\T,
\end{equation}
which has rank at most $r$.  If $P^\T v=0$, the same expression immediately
gives $Tv=\eta v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the scalar complement is part of the model]
\label{rem:complement}
Without the complement, a conventional form $\I_N+PAP^\T$ fixes the entire
$\ker(P^\T)$ subspace to gain one.  Equation~\eqref{eq:defect-completion}
instead assigns that unmodeled subspace the learned per-head scalar $\eta$,
while retaining the same rank-$r$ correction and the strict contraction
certificate.  This does not make $T$ an arbitrary full-rank attention map:
outside the represented subspace it remains isotropic.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Padding isolation]
\label{prop:padding}
If an invalid row has $A_i=C_i=0$, then its soft-frame row is zero and its
pre-output coordinate $Y_i$ is zero.  Consequently, explicit output masking
preserves a zero value for every invalid token.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper block of the QR factor spans the columns of $A$, so a zero row of
$A$ produces a zero row of $P$.  With $C_i=0$, the $i$th row of
\eqref{eq:equilibrium-readout} is therefore zero.  The final output mask
retains that value after the feature projection.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Noncausal permutation equivariance]
\label{prop:permutation-equivariance}
Let $\Pi$ be a permutation of token rows.  If tokens, validity mask, and
position identifiers are permuted together, then one LSSO head obeys
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{LSSO}(\Pi X,\Pi m,\Pi p)
    =\Pi\operatorname{LSSO}(X,m,p).
\label{eq:permutation-equivariance}
\end{equation}
Thus the global compact statistic has no causal scan order or inner-mini-batch
ordering.  Position identifiers still carry whatever absolute or relative
coordinate semantics the caller assigns to them.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All rowwise projections, masking, normalization, and rank-space rotations
commute with the synchronized permutation, so $A'=\Pi A$ and $C'=\Pi C$.
The positive-diagonal reduced QR factorization is unique.  Applying the block
orthogonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\Pi,\I_r)$ to
$[A^\T\;\I_r]^\T$ therefore gives $P'=\Pi P$ with the same lower block and
$R_{\mathrm{qr}}$.  Consequently
$Z'=P'^{\T}C'=P^\T C=Z$, so the dynamic compact coordinates, $K$, $M$, and
$\eta$ are unchanged.  Equation~\eqref{eq:token-mixer} then gives
$Y'=\Pi Y$; the rowwise output projection preserves the relation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Scope of the certificate}
\label{sec:theory-scope}

Theorems~\ref{thm:accretive-cayley} and \ref{thm:token-contraction} are
deliberately conditional: for every input, once the relation frame and compact
core generated by that input are frozen, the resulting linear token mixer is
strictly contractive.  They do \emph{not} prove that the map
$X\mapsto T(X)C(X)$ is globally Lipschitz.  Its Jacobian also contains the
derivatives of the input projection, QR factorization, dynamic compact
coordinates, output projection, residual connection, and surrounding MLP.
Nor do the theorems imply a global bound for a full residual block or network.

The result also makes no claim to approximate every softmax attention matrix.
The structural restriction $\rank(T-\eta\I_N)\leq r$ is explicit.  LSSO is
noncausal and global, not a streaming state-space model with constant-size
autoregressive inference.  Finally, Rank-Rotary is only an optional
rank-feature phase ablation; learned image positional embeddings or other
backbone position mechanisms remain responsible for spatial coordinates.

The production implementation evaluates the same equations with a documented
mixed TF32/TC16 contract for supported CUDA paths and an FP64 reference oracle
for validation.  The exact theorems explain the intended normal-training
geometry; they are not a bitwise statement about arbitrarily extreme
floating-point raw coordinates.

\section{Sequence Classification Evidence}
\label{sec:sequence-experiments}

We first test whether the certified mixer remains trainable and useful when it
replaces attention in otherwise ordinary bidirectional encoders.  All reported
LSSO runs use the default \textsc{Dynamic} core, Rank-Rotary, rank $32$, FP16
automatic mixed precision, and the CUDA implementation.  Checkpoints are
selected only by validation accuracy, with validation loss breaking an exact
tie; the held-out test split is evaluated once after selection.  Reported
uncertainties are sample standard deviations over seeds $0$, $1$, and $2$.

\section{Limitations and Required Validation}
\label{sec:limitations}

The current theory certifies each sample-conditioned token kernel after its
contextual quantities are frozen.  It does not certify the full nonlinear
network, guarantee task improvement, recover arbitrary dense attention, or
provide causal constant-state decoding.  The genomic comparison supports a
matched replacement claim, whereas the LRA literature table does not support
a cross-paper ranking.

Several empirical questions remain necessary for a complete evaluation.
First, the three core ownership modes and the Rank-Rotary switch must be
ablated under one fixed shell.  Second, finite unrolls of
\eqref{eq:contextual-flow} or \eqref{eq:residual-objective} should be compared
with the direct solve in forward error, gradient error, wall time, and peak
memory; this directly tests the solved-inner-process interpretation.  Third,
sequence-length and rank sweeps should report wall time and memory against MHA,
together with training-time measurements of the generated contraction margin,
$\sigma_{\min}(\I+K)$, and solve residual.  Finally, the planned ImageNet,
COCO, and ADE20K experiments are needed before making a broad vision-backbone
claim.  These are evidence requirements, not additional operator variants.

\section{Conclusion}

LSSO replaces an unrolled contextual adaptation process with the direct
solution of a compact strictly accretive system.  The construction combines
noncausal global conditioning, a complete chart of strict compact
contractions, and a per-sample frozen-kernel certificate while retaining
linear sequence scaling at fixed rank.  The exact solve is both the
infinite-time limit of a globally convergent inner flow and the infinite-step
limit of stable residual gradient descent, but requires neither process in the
implemented forward pass.  Initial sequence results show that this structure
is trainable and can improve a matched attention baseline; the remaining
experiments above delimit what must still be established empirically.
